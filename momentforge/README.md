# MomentForge

A record of MomentForge, an AI product by Joshua Thomas, kept in the open as it is built. This page states what it is, how it works, and where it honestly stands. Nothing here claims a user or a result that has not happened.

## The principle

AI should help a person live a meaningful experience, not maximize the time that person spends chatting with the AI. When a real world action is worth more than another exchange, the system helps the person take the action and then gets out of the way. Meaning is pushed outward, toward real people, relationships, creativity, community, work, places, and experiences. The AI never positions itself as the person's most important relationship.

## The loop

Remember, Dream, Choose, Act, Leave, Reflect, Update.

Remember what matters. Dream one feasible moment. Choose the version the person wants. Act on the smallest real step. Leave the screen and live it. Reflect on what actually happened. Update the person's own narrative with what came back.

## The parts it reasons from

- Anchor: a person, relationship, craft, place, community, responsibility, purpose, or ideal that makes life feel meaningful.
- Why: what the anchor represents to the person.
- Direction: the kind of life, or the kind of person, the user wants to move toward.
- Dream: something the user imagines experiencing, creating, becoming, contributing, or sharing.
- Moment: a specific real world experience that could occur.
- Action: the smallest feasible behavior that could cause that moment.
- Memory: the user's record of what actually happened.
- Meaning: the interpretation of why the experience mattered.

## The measure

The north star metric is meaningful real world moments created per user per week. Not messages sent, not session length, not time in the app. Secondary measures, once real use exists, may include how many committed moments were attempted, how many the person found meaningful afterward, and whether the moment would have happened anyway. Until testing produces real numbers, these are proposed measures, not results.

- No users are claimed. No results are claimed.

## The physical test

The first test is physical, not digital. A paper Return Card is handed to a person in a room. The front is filled in together in under two minutes: one anchor, the smallest action, and the time the person will leave to do it. The back returns with what actually happened, why it mattered in the person's own words, and a mark from the real world, such as the other person's initials or a date. Five cards go out first, to people the builder already knows, and returns are counted over seven days, failures included. The printable cards stand in this folder as return-cards.html, and what returns will be counted in the open, on the Work page and in the progress log.
